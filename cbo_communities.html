Program Overview

What is a CBO Community?

CBO Hub offers capacity building programs designed to extend the reach of church resources — enabling congregations to serve their local neighborhoods at greater scale and with greater consistency.

Our purpose is singular: to build active and sustainable CBO communities by connecting faith-based organizations with the institutional partners and service infrastructure they need.

Reaching underserved populations is difficult. Systems are fragmented. Resources exist but remain disconnected from the people who need them most. CBO Communities resolve this by creating structured collaboration between the organizations already embedded in these neighborhoods.

As a CBO Host, your church becomes the anchor — equipping members to serve local neighborhoods through an organized network of community-based organizations working in coordination.

The CBO Member Process

Five steps from enrollment
to active community.

Each step is sequenced to build on the last — from honest internal assessment to a fully operational community network serving your local neighborhood.

Step One

Assessment

Take the structured assessment to identify your organization's current strengths and gaps. Honest evaluation at this stage ensures the sustainability plan is grounded in operational reality rather than aspiration.

1
2
Step Two

Sustainability Plan

Define what is operationally necessary to sustain your location for the next three years. This plan becomes the financial and programmatic foundation upon which your service strategy is built.

Step Three

Service Plan

Select a primary offering based on your location and the greatest unmet need in the community you serve. Determine whether you will extend your own capacity or host an organization with existing service infrastructure.

3
4
Step Four

Build Your CBO Community

Invite CBO Partners to serve as your service provider or anchor. These partners collaborate directly with your church to deliver critical services — operating as an extension of your mission, not in spite of it.

Step Five

Capacitate Members

Equip congregation members to serve local neighborhoods through CBO Community collaboration and available resources. Members with first-hand knowledge of local conditions serve as trusted ambassadors — expanding program reach through credibility and proximity.

Program Advantages

Why CBO Communities
delivers where others do not.

01

Proximity as Infrastructure

Church members who live and work in the neighborhoods being served carry irreplaceable local knowledge. CBO Communities turn that proximity into a strategic asset, not an afterthought.

02

Structured Collaboration

Rather than informal partnerships, CBO Communities creates formal coordination frameworks between churches, public agencies, philanthropic bodies, and service organizations — reducing duplication and increasing service reach.

03

Capacity Without Overhead

Churches can choose to host organizations with existing capacity rather than build services from scratch. This model extends impact without requiring proportional growth in staff, facilities, or budget.

04

3-Year Sustainability Framework

The mandatory Sustainability Plan ensures every participating church enters the network with a realistic operating horizon — protecting the community from organizations that cannot sustain their commitments.

05

Need-Driven Service Design

Service plans are built from assessed local need rather than organizational preference. This ensures resources flow toward the actual gaps in each specific community — not generic program templates.

06

Trusted Ambassador Network

Capacitated members become community ambassadors — bridging the trust deficit that prevents underserved populations from engaging with institutional services, even those designed to help them.

Members who live in the local area have first-hand knowledge of local needs and serve as trusted ambassadors for the programs you offer.
